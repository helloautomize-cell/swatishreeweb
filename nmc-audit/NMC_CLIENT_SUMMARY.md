# Your website and the new NMC advertising rules

**For Dr. Swati Shree — one page, plain language**

India's medical regulator (the NMC) has new rules about what doctors and clinics may say on their websites and in public communication. We checked the whole EVE website against them before launch. The good news first.

## What is already right

Your site was built with these rules in mind. There are **no patient testimonials or reviews, no star ratings, no success-rate claims, no before/after photos, no patient stories, no prices or discount offers**. Every treatment page explains risks and says plainly that results vary. Every page says the content is not a substitute for a consultation, shows your Karnataka Medical Council registration number, and names you as the medical reviewer. The appointment form asks for consent properly, Google Analytics only runs if a visitor agrees, and the map loads only when clicked. We did not need to invent any of this — it was already done.

## What needs to change

Three items need work, and five need information from you.

1. **The footer credit "Built & Marketed by Automize Media Labs" must go.** The rules do not allow a doctor to publicly engage a marketing agency, and that line says exactly that on every page. We will either remove it or reduce it to a neutral "Website: …" credit.

2. **Your photo is used too prominently.** The rules allow a plain photo on your profile page, but not portraits used to promote the clinic. Your photo currently leads the homepage, appears again in the "Meet your doctor" section, and in small avatars across the site. We will keep one good photo on your profile page and use clinic imagery on the homepage instead.

3. **One photo needs checking.** A consultation photo on the About page shows a couple whose faces are clearly visible. If they are patients, we need written consent on file; if they are models or staff, we need that confirmed — or we swap to a photo where the patient cannot be identified. Please tell us who they are.

## What we need from you

- **The GCU Women's Day Award**: the year it was received and on what basis, plus something that verifies it (a certificate, a university page). Otherwise we remove it.
- **Your National ART and Surrogacy Registry number** and confirmation of the clinic's exact registration scope. Several blog posts say "ART Level 1 certified clinic" — we will fix the wording once we know the facts, or remove the claim if registration sits with the partner centres.
- **How you want the IVF arrangement described**: should Apollo Fertility and Motherhood Fertility be named as where lab work happens, or should the site say "associated, registered ART centres"? Our recommendation: name them once, factually, on the IVF page only.
- **Privacy details**: the privacy contact and grievance officer (name, email, phone), how long enquiry data is kept, and the clinic email address.
- **Two approvals in writing**: (a) the wording that says pages are drafted with AI writing tools and reviewed and approved by you — the new rules want this disclosed; (b) your personal story on the About page about your son's premature birth.
- **One small confirmation**: the exact registration number string you want shown (your certificate reads "DLH 2009 0000353 KTK"; the renewal shows "0000353").

## Also worth knowing

- A handful of blog posts have stray editing marks ("Pasted text…") and a few say procedures are "painless" — we will clean both up.
- Your `/styleguide/` developer page will be locked before launch.
- Your Google Business Profile and WhatsApp Business listing should follow the same rules — no asking for reviews, no offers, no superlatives. When you are ready to connect social accounts, the same applies there.

## The bottom line

Nothing found is a serious violation — there are no cure promises, no fake reviews, no patient data exposed. The site is in strong shape; these changes are about tightening wording, photos and disclosures before anything goes public. Most fixes are small once your confirmations arrive.
